## Finding 1: AI Overview Visibility Varies 12x by Topic — Health 60%, Breaking News Just 5.4%
**Details:** New data from Newzdash analyzed how frequently AI Overviews appear across Google search result sections. The variance is dramatic: Health queries trigger AI Overviews 60.5% of the time, Technology 38.2%, Business 36.6%, Entertainment 24.2%, Sports 21.5%, World news 16.9%, National news 14.4% — but Breaking News and Major Headlines only 5.4% of the time. This is a 12x difference between the most and least AI-summarized categories. The data, shared by John Shehata on LinkedIn, was based on a channel-level breakdown across 2,500+ searches. The implication: AI Overviews are not a universal SERP feature — topic selection is now a direct AI visibility strategy. Publishers in high-AIO categories (health, tech, business) face a different competitive dynamic than news publishers who largely bypass AI summarization.
**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable / Newzdash

## Finding 2: Call Buttons Vanishing from Local Packs — 4 out of 5 Map Pack Results Now Lack One
**Details:** A study from Smallbiz Edge analyzed 172 keywords across 15 locations (2,580 total searches) and found that call buttons appear in Local Packs only about 20% of the time — meaning 4 out of 5 map pack results have no call button. Google has been progressively removing call buttons from local packs over the past year. Joy Hawkins noted this trend on X, flagging that phone number prominence in business listings is now more critical than ever. For local SEO practitioners, this signals that the phone number itself must be highly visible in business descriptions and on the landing page, since users can no longer rely on a one-tap call button in the local pack. Alternative conversion paths (driving directions, WhatsApp, messaging) need to be front-and-center.
**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable / Smallbiz Edge / Joy Hawkins

## Finding 3: Google "Developing" Opt-Out for Generative AI Features in Search (UK CMA Response)
**Details:** Google submitted a response to the UK's Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) proposing new digital market rules. In it, Google stated it is "developing" a mechanism to let website owners opt out of having their content used in generative AI features in Search (AI Overviews, AI Mode). Google wrote: "Our goal is to make sure website owners have the right controls to manage how their content is used." This is the first formal acknowledgment from Google that an AI opt-out mechanism is being built. For SEO practitioners, this creates a new technical requirement: monitoring for any future robots.txt directives, meta tags, or search console settings related to AI content usage. It also signals that regulatory pressure is becoming a genuine forcing function for AI transparency in search.
**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable / Google CMA Response
**Date:** March 18, 2026

## Finding 4: Google Removes "What People Suggest" Health SERP Feature After One Year
**Details:** Google quietly removed the "What People Suggest" SERP feature — which organized and surfaced health conversations from Reddit, X, Facebook, and other social media using AI — as part of a "broad simplification" of search results. The feature launched in March 2025 and was removed in March 2026. Google confirmed the removal to The Guardian, stating it had "nothing to do with the quality or safety of the feature." The removal means one less SERP real estate opportunity for forum-based and social content in the health vertical. Google's official statement noted they will continue to help people find reliable health information "including forums with first-person perspectives" — but the dedicated SERP placement for these is now gone. This creates an interesting dynamic: social/forum content quality signals remain important for AI citation (since Google still reads forums) but those citations no longer get a dedicated visual placement.
**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable / The Guardian
**Date:** March 17, 2026
**Actionability Score:** 7

## Finding 5: Google Business Profiles Gains "Place Page Attributes" Section
**Details:** Google added a new section to Google Business Profiles called "Place page attributes" that allows business owners to display additional attributes about their business publicly on Search, Maps, and other Google services. These attributes appear as a new category in GBP settings, distinct from existing attribute types. This is a direct expansion of local business signals on Google's primary business listing surface. SEO practitioners managing multi-location businesses should audit their GBP panels for this new section and populate it with relevant attributes — these now appear across multiple Google surfaces, not just the classic GBP interface. It's a new on-platform signal that can differentiate listings in competitive local markets.

## Finding 6: Google Using AI to Create Title Links on SERP (Not Just AI Overviews)
**Details:** Google confirmed it is using AI to generate title links directly on the standard SERP — not just within AI Overviews. This means AI-generated headlines are appearing for all web pages in regular search results, not just in AI-heavy interfaces. This is an escalation of the AI title rewriting phenomenon noted in earlier rounds. Google is now applying its language model headline generation to standard organic results, replacing or supplementing the HTML `<title>` tag. SEO practitioners should: (1) monitor GSC Performance reports for sudden title tag changes, (2) ensure page titles remain descriptive and match actual content (as Google may rewrite titles that don't accurately reflect page content), and (3) test titles that are compact and descriptive, since AI models may prefer shorter, clearer headlines.

## Finding 8: Google Confirms New "Google-Agent" User Agent for Web Crawling
**Details:** Google officially announced a new user-agent called "Google-Agent" which is "rolling out over the next few weeks" and will be used by "Google agents hosted on Google infrastructure to navigate the web and perform actions upon user request." This is a formal crawler designation separate from Googlebot. It confirms that Google is deploying agentic web crawling — automated agents that don't just index pages but perform actions on behalf of users. From an SEO technical perspective: (1) webmasters should ensure robots.txt allows Google-Agent if they want their content accessible to Google agents, (2) site speed and API responsiveness may become a ranking-adjacent factor if agents are evaluating pages in real-time, and (3) structured data that helps agents understand page actions (e.g., potentialAction, SpeakableSpecification) may gain importance.
**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable / Google

## Finding 10: ChatGPT Ads Offer Zero Performance Transparency — OpenAI Has "No Automated Ad Buying"
**Details:** The Information reported that OpenAI's first ChatGPT advertisers cannot prove the ads drive measurable business outcomes. OpenAI's ad platform is reportedly "low tech" — requiring advertisers to buy ad space via phone calls, spreadsheets, and emails rather than self-serve portals. There is no automated ad buying, no performance data sharing, and limited targeting features. OpenAI has announced plans to expand ads to all US free-tier users "in the coming weeks." Glenn Gabe summarized: advertisers need to provide more creative variations to improve performance but have zero visibility into what's actually working. For SEO-CRO practitioners, this signals that ChatGPT advertising is not yet a credible traffic or conversion channel — attribution models that include ChatGPT referrals should be treated with extreme skepticism until OpenAI provides transparent reporting. The organic discovery implications (will branded content in ChatGPT conversations drive traffic?) remain unclear and unmeasurable.

## Finding 11: SEO Volatility Outside Core Updates — "Smaller Core Updates" Causing Major Ranking Swings in Q1 2026
**Details:** Glenn Gabe reported on X that Q1 2026 has been "super volatile" with ranking movements happening outside the official broad core update schedule. Google told Search Engine Roundtable that they can push "smaller core updates" at any time outside of the broad core update announcements. This means the traditional "wait for the broad core update to finish" framework is outdated — Google is running continuous ranking experiments and smaller quality-related updates throughout the year. Glenn noted: "Google can push what they call 'smaller core updates' outside of broad core updates, and those can still have a big impact." For SEO practitioners, this requires: (1) more frequent traffic monitoring (weekly rather than monthly), (2) faster investigation of unexplained ranking changes, and (3) client communications that account for rolling volatility as the new normal rather than update-event-driven volatility.
