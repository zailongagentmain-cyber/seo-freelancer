## Finding 1: Google Search Live Goes Global — Gemini 3.1 Flash Live Powers Real-Time Voice Search

### Details
Google announced that **Search Live** has launched globally, for all languages and locations where AI Mode is available. This expansion is powered by Google's new audio and voice model, **Gemini 3.1 Flash Live**, which delivers "more natural and intuitive conversations" than previous models. With this expansion, people in more than 200 countries and territories can have real-time conversations with Search in AI Mode, using both voice and camera. Google emphasized that the new model is "inherently multilingual," meaning users around the world can speak with Search in their preferred language — a significant leap from earlier voice search constrained by language limitations. To use Search Live, users open the Google app on Android or iOS and tap the Live icon under the Search bar, then ask questions aloud to receive audio responses with helpful web links for follow-up conversations.

### Source
Search Engine Roundtable / Google Blog / Rajan Patel (Twitter/X)
**Date:** March 26–27, 2026 (announced and expanded)
**Actionability Score: 8/10**
> Optimize your content for voice search queries in multiple languages — this global expansion means international voice search volume will grow significantly. Ensure your FAQ and conversational content sections address natural language questions users would speak aloud. Structured data for FAQs and How-To content becomes even more important as voice search surfaces direct answers. Consider multilingual SEO as Search Live removes the language barrier for AI Mode voice interactions.

## Finding 2: Google Ads PMax Gets First-Party Audience Exclusions + New Reporting Suite

### Details
Google announced several major new steering updates for **Google Ads Performance Max (PMax)**. The headline feature is **first-party audience exclusions** — enabling advertisers to exclude specific customer lists from their Performance Max campaigns. This allows advertisers to focus budget on acquiring new customers rather than re-engaging those who have already converted, solving a long-standing pain point with PMax. Google also introduced three new insight reports: (1) **Budget reporting** — projects end-of-month spend directly within PMax to help advertisers understand how shifting daily budgets impacts performance; (2) **Network segmentation in placement reporting** — allows advertisers to see exactly where ads served, segmented by network (YouTube, Search, Display, etc.), with brand safety visibility; and (3) **Full audience reporting** — detailed performance breakdowns by audience demographics including age range and gender. These updates collectively give advertisers significantly more control and visibility into PMax campaigns.

### Source
Search Engine Roundtable / Google Business Blog
**Date:** March 27, 2026
**Actionability Score: 8/10**
> Upload your existing customer list to Google Ads and configure first-party audience exclusions in your PMax campaigns immediately. Use the new budget reporting to model end-of-month scenarios before peak spending periods. Review network segmentation data to identify placements that may be consuming budget without driving conversions — apply exclusions accordingly. Segment audience reporting by demographics to identify underperforming demographic groups that may need creative or bidding adjustments.

## Finding 3: Bing Tests Rounded Corners on Short Videos in Search Results

### Details
Microsoft Bing is testing a new visual UI treatment for short videos and standard videos within Bing search results: **rounded corners** replacing the previous squared-off edges. This is a subtle but meaningful visual refresh that aligns Bing's video thumbnail presentation more closely with modern mobile-first design patterns and other platforms (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram all use rounded corners). The change affects both short-form video content and longer-form video results, making the SERP appearance more consistent across content types. While this is primarily a UI/design test, it signals Bing's continued investment in the visual presentation of its search results and its push to compete more aggressively for video-based search queries.

## Finding 5: Google Agent User Agent — AI Agent Traffic Now Identifiable in Server Logs

### Details
Search Engine Land reported that Google has introduced a specific **user agent pattern for Google Agent** — the AI-powered agentic browsing and action system within Google's ecosystem. This means webmasters and server administrators can now identify **AI agent traffic** in their server logs by detecting the specific Google Agent user agent string. Previously, Google's AI agent traffic was difficult to distinguish from regular Googlebot crawling. Now, with the dedicated user agent, site operators can: (1) accurately measure how much of their traffic comes from Google's AI agents versus human visitors, (2) analyze which pages Google's agents are accessing and taking actions on, and (3) make informed decisions about allowing or blocking AI agent access to specific content. This development reflects the growing importance of AI-mediated traffic and consumption of web content — where AI agents browse, summarize, and act on behalf of users.

### Source
Search Engine Land
**Date:** March 27, 2026 (within the reporting period)
**Actionability Score: 7/10**
> Check your server logs for the new Google Agent user agent string and segment this traffic separately in your analytics. Understand how much of your content consumption is AI-agent driven versus human. Review which pages are being accessed by Google Agent and ensure those pages have complete, accurate structured data and schema markup. If you use robots.txt or meta tags to control AI agent access, audit these settings now that you can clearly see AI agent traffic volumes.

## Finding 6: EU Backs Nude App Ban and Delays Implementation of Landmark AI Act

### Details
The European Union has backed a ban on nude image generation apps ("nudify" apps) and simultaneously announced delays to the phased implementation of its comprehensive **AI Act** — the world's first binding AI regulation framework. The delay in AI Act implementation affects certain high-risk AI system deadlines, amid pushback from industry stakeholders who argue the compliance timelines are too aggressive. The nude app ban specifically targets apps that use AI to remove clothing from photos of real people, which have proliferated rapidly with the rise of diffusion models. The EU's action signals that it will aggressively regulate harmful AI applications even while adjusting timelines for broader AI compliance obligations. The delay in AI Act timelines creates uncertainty for companies preparing for high-risk AI system obligations under the act's Annex III classifications.

### Source
The Verge / Search Engine Roundtable (March 27, 2026)
**Date:** March 27, 2026 (within the reporting period)
**Actionability Score: 7/10**
> If your company operates AI-enabled products or services in the EU, monitor the revised AI Act implementation timeline closely. The delay may provide additional runway for compliance preparation — use it wisely. Ensure your AI systems have appropriate data governance, transparency, and human oversight measures, especially if classified as high-risk under the AI Act. If you operate in the content/moderation space, review the nude app ban requirements and ensure your platforms comply with the EU's AI-generated content rules.

## Finding 7: OpenAI Surpasses $100M Annualized Revenue From Ads Pilot

### Details

OpenAI began testing ads within ChatGPT in late 2024/early 2025, and the revenue trajectory suggests advertisers are finding value in reaching users within the AI chat environment. This milestone also underscores the broader shift in digital advertising budgets toward AI-native platforms and chat-based interfaces, as AI assistants become a primary content consumption channel.
