Googlebot Crawling 404 Pages: The Surprising SEO Signal You Need to Know

Updated: 2026-03-26 | By 龙雅人

In a surprising turn of events, Google's John Mueller has revealed something that goes against conventional SEO wisdom: Googlebot crawling your 404 pages is actually a positive sign.

What John Mueller Actually Said

During a recent Google SEO office hours session, Mueller clarified that seeing Googlebot hitting 404 pages isn't a problem—it's actually an indicator that Google is willing to crawl more of your content.

"Seeing 404s in your logs means Googlebot is trying to crawl more of your site. That's a good thing."

This statement flips traditional SEO advice on its head. For years, site owners have panicked when they see 404 errors in their crawl reports, believing it signals crawl budget waste or technical problems.

Why 404 Pages Are Actually Positive

1. Google Wants More Content

When Googlebot encounters 404s, it means the bot is actively discovering new URLs on your site. This indicates:

  • Your site has fresh content worth crawling
  • Google considers your site important enough to explore thoroughly
  • The crawl budget isn't being wasted on low-value pages

2. It's About Quality, Not Quantity

Mueller emphasized that Google's crawling behavior is driven by content quality, not technical perfection. A few 404 errors won't hurt your rankings if your core content is solid.

3. Focus on What Matters

Instead of obsessing over every 404, focus on:

  • Ensuring important pages return 200 status codes
  • Having a clean site structure
  • Creating high-quality, valuable content
  • Properly redirecting moved content (301 redirects)

What This Means for Your SEO Strategy

Don't Panic Over 404s

Here's the bottom line: occasional 404 errors are normal and even healthy. They're a sign that your site is being actively crawled and indexed.

Proper 404 Handling

That said, you should still handle 404s properly:

1. Custom 404 Page: Create a helpful 404 page that keeps users engaged 2. Noindex Directive: If you must have 404 pages, don't block them in robots.txt 3. Internal Linking: Ensure your important pages are well-linked 4. Redirect Strategy: Use 301 redirects for permanently moved content

Monitor, Don't Obsess

Use tools like Google Search Console to monitor your 404 patterns. Look for:

  • Spike in 404s (might indicate a technical issue)
  • 404s on important pages (needs immediate attention)
  • Old 404s from removed content (normal, leave them)

The Bigger Picture

This revelation aligns with Google's broader messaging: focus on content quality rather than technical perfection. The algorithm is smart enough to understand that:

  • Not every page needs to exist forever
  • Content naturally expires or becomes outdated
  • A few technical imperfections won't tank your rankings

Conclusion

The next time you see 404 errors in your crawl logs, don't panic. Remember: Googlebot crawling your 404 pages means Google is open to more of your content.

Focus on creating valuable content, maintaining a clean site structure, and properly handling moved or deleted pages. Let Google worry about crawl budget optimization—you focus on what matters: your audience and your content.
