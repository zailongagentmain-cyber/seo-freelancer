## The March 2026 Core Update at Week Two

The March 2026 Core Update began rolling out on March 27, 2026, and Google indicated it would take up to two weeks to complete. As of April 6, we are at the midpoint — and the first wave of fallout reports is revealing patterns.

Glenn Gabe has been tracking the movement closely, and his observation captures the core tension: **"AI Overviews are showing for breaking news more often and above top stories."** This is not a subtle change. For years, breaking news queries were prime real estate for publishers. Now Google's own AI system is claiming that real estate, pushing traditional news rankings below the fold.

Roger Montti notes that the March spam update's proximity was likely not coincidental. The spam update — which completed in under 20 hours on March 24–25 — eliminated the lowest-quality content at scale. The core update that followed has been sweeping up sites that survived the spam filter but still lack genuine substance.

The wave-like volatility pattern continues because core updates don't follow a single deployment mechanism. Different teams at Google run different systems, contributing changes at different times. The result is a rolling recalibration that can produce rank movements for weeks after the official "completion" date.

Google's official guidance remains unchanged: wait at least one full week after the rollout finishes before analyzing Search Console data. That means the first meaningful dataset won't be available until approximately April 17. Sites still showing pre-December 2025 patterns should expect movement by mid-April.

Early reports suggest the steepest declines are hitting **informational content sites with high AI Overviews presence** — the same sites that benefited from AI Overviews traffic in 2024–2025 are now getting penalized as Google expands AI Overviews coverage. Sites with clear E-E-A-T signals, original reporting, and first-person expertise are holding or gaining.

## Google Zero: The 65% No-Click Reality

The zero-click search phenomenon — where a Google search ends without any click to an organic or paid result — has reached 55–65% of all Google searches, up from approximately 50% in 2025. This is not a temporary fluctuation. It is a structural shift driven by AI Overviews, featured snippets, knowledge panels, and direct answer boxes that satisfy search intent without requiring a click.

The publisher impact has been severe. Sites that depend on informational organic traffic have reported **double-digit percentage drops in sessions** for queries where AI Overviews now appear prominently. The traffic that remains is increasingly coming from branded queries, transactional searches, and long-tail queries where Google's AI hasn't yet developed confidence to serve a direct answer.

Search Engine Land published a critical analysis arguing that the "Google Zero" conversation has been focused on the wrong metric. The real problem isn't that publishers aren't getting clicks — it's that **the next visitor isn't human**. AI agents are reading and processing web content at massive scale, but they don't click. They cite. This shifts the value proposition of SEO from driving human clicks to earning AI citations.

The strategic implication is clear: **brands need to optimize for AI citation, not just human click-through**. GEO — Generative Engine Optimization — is the practice of structuring and writing content so that AI search engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews) cite it as a authoritative source. This means clear answers to specific questions, strong E-E-A-T signals, and content that AI engines can confidently extract and attribute.

## Yoast Ships llms.txt for Shopify — The First Major E-Commerce Integration

On March 31, 2026, Yoast released an update to their Shopify app that includes native llms.txt support — the first major e-commerce platform integration for the spec that was proposed by developer Arent-Jan Blankendaal and has been gaining adoption since late 2024.

The llms.txt file provides AI crawlers with a structured map of a site's content, product categories, and data usage policies. For Shopify stores, this means:

- Store owners can configure **what content is available for AI training and citation** and what is reserved
- Product information, pricing, and inventory can be **selectively exposed or withheld** from AI crawlers
- The commercial dimension of llms.txt is addressed — shopping-specific signals like availability, price, and specifications can be structured for AI agents that operate shopping tasks

This follows the pattern established by Craft.ai, various headless CMS platforms, and the growing llms.txt Hub directory (838 commits as of mid-March 2026) that tracks sites implementing the spec. Yoast's implementation represents the first time a major e-commerce platform has offered llms.txt as a native feature rather than a custom implementation.

As AI shopping agents become more prevalent — Walmart's AI checkout already operates at one-third the rate of regular e-commerce — having structured product data available for AI citation will shift from a technical nice-to-have to a competitive necessity.

## Grokipedia: The Latest 'Mt. AI' Cautionary Tale

Barry Schwartz documented Grokipedia's collapse in both traditional Google search and AI search visibility. The site initially surged heavily after scaling with AI-generated content — a classic "Mt. AI" pattern that has now become familiar:

1. Site scales content production with AI tools
2. Initial surge in Google rankings (AI-scaled content appears comprehensive)
3. Google's quality systems catch up — spam update, core update, or AI-specific quality signal
4. **Steep drop in both traditional search AND AI search visibility**

Grokipedia's decline is a data point in a growing pattern of sites that attempted to game AI scaling without the E-E-A-T scaffolding to support it. Google's March spam update likely triggered the initial drop, with the core update compounding the effect.

The pattern reinforces something fundamental about content quality: **original, human-authored content with genuine expertise signals is more durable than AI-scaled content that lacks those signals**. Google's quality systems — both traditional and AI-specific — are increasingly capable of distinguishing between content that is comprehensive and content that merely appears comprehensive.

## AI Search Citation Patterns: Reddit, YouTube, LinkedIn Dominate

A study of AI search engine citation patterns found that Reddit, YouTube, and LinkedIn are the most frequently cited sources by AI search engines — ChatGPT with browsing, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. The pattern reflects the platforms' combination of scale, freshness, and perceived authority:

- **Reddit's Q&A format** provides clear, direct answers — ideal for AI citation because it eliminates ambiguity
- **YouTube's video transcripts** provide authoritative how-to content that AI engines can parse into structured text
- **LinkedIn's professional content** carries strong authority signals for business and technical queries

Traditional publisher sites — news outlets, marketing blogs, SEO publications — are cited less frequently. When they are cited, it is often with **partial extracts rather than comprehensive answers**, suggesting AI engines prefer sources that directly answer specific questions rather than provide comprehensive overviews.

The implication for content strategy is significant: **Q&A format content, technical how-tos, and first-person professional perspectives are more likely to be cited by AI engines** than traditional SEO-optimized overview articles. The shift is away from comprehensive "ultimate guides" toward focused, expert-level answers to specific questions.

A study of SEO job listings found that 59% of positions are now senior-level roles — Senior SEO Manager, Head of SEO, Director of SEO, and similar. Entry-level SEO positions have declined sharply as AI tools have automated basic SEO tasks like keyword research, meta tag optimization, and basic content optimization.

This structural shift reflects the same pattern happening in content creation: **AI is automating the routine, and humans are being pushed up the value chain**. The remaining entry-level SEO work has shifted toward AI supervision, content strategy, and technical SEO — all roles that require more experience and judgment than the keyword-stuffing and meta-tag-writing of the 2010s.

Companies are increasingly hiring **SEO specialists with AI and GEO skills** rather than traditional SEO-only practitioners. The professional distinction between "SEO" and "content marketing" is blurring, with both fields now requiring fluency in AI tooling, prompt engineering, and generative engine optimization.

## Technical SEO for AI Agents: A New Discipline

Search Engine Land published a comprehensive guide on "Technical SEO for Generative Search: Optimizing for AI Agents." As AI agents — not just chatbots but systems that browse and interact with websites autonomously — become more prevalent as content consumers, a new form of technical SEO is emerging.

AI agents have different browsing patterns than humans:

- They follow links systematically rather than navigating visually
- They parse structured data (JSON-LD) as a primary content signal
- They evaluate site architecture for credibility and crawlability
- They make "trust" decisions based on signals humans can't see

Key technical factors for AI agent optimization include:

- **Clear, hierarchical site structure** — AI agents navigate information architecture, not visual design
- **Complete JSON-LD structured data** — schema.org markup is a primary content signal for AI agents
- **Fast, lightweight pages** — AI agents have byte budgets similar to Googlebot; heavy pages may be truncated
- **Clean HTML** — heavy JavaScript rendering requirements can cause AI agents to miss content
- **Consistent canonical signals** — contradictory canonical tags confuse AI agents more than humans

The emerging MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers are also relevant here. As AI agents use MCP to discover and interact with content, the infrastructure that supports MCP may become as important as traditional XML sitemaps.

## Google Ask Maps: AI Goes Local

Google's "Ask Maps" feature — allowing users to ask conversational questions about locations, directions, and local business information — reached full availability in the U.S. and India on April 1, 2026. Users can now ask questions like "Is this restaurant good for a business lunch?" or "What's a good route that avoids tolls?" and receive AI-generated answers.

This represents Google's push to **integrate AI conversational interfaces across core search products** — not just AI Overviews in the main SERP but Maps, Shopping, and other surfaces. For local SEO, the implication is significant:

- Conversational local queries will increasingly be answered directly by Google's AI rather than showing a list of business results
- Reviews, business descriptions, and structured local content become the raw material for AI-generated local answers
- The **Google Business Profile optimization** takes on new importance as a source signal for AI-generated local responses

The line between traditional local SEO and AI-optimized local presence is disappearing. The same signals that ranked well in the local pack — reviews, completeness, authority signals — are now feeding AI-generated answers.

OpenAI's announcement that it closed a funding round at a $852 billion valuation is significant for SEO professionals not because of the number, but because of what it signals about the competitive landscape:

- **AI-native search will continue to grow** — OpenAI's ChatGPT with real-time browsing is an increasingly credible alternative to Google for informational queries

## What This Means for Your SEO Strategy

The developments in this round point toward several strategic adjustments:

**1. GEO is now urgent, not theoretical.** The 65% zero-click rate means optimizing for human clicks alone is insufficient. Content must be structured to earn AI citations — clear Q&A formats, specific answers to specific questions, strong E-E-A-T signals.

**2. Quality over quantity is no longer a platitude.** The Grokipedia pattern demonstrates that AI-scaled thin content gets corrected — eventually. The sites gaining from the March 2026 Core Update are those with genuine expertise and original reporting.

**3. Technical SEO for AI agents is an emerging requirement.** JSON-LD completeness, clean HTML, fast pages, and consistent canonical signals help both Googlebot and AI agents. The skills are converging.

**4. Multi-channel AI presence matters.** Reddit, YouTube, and LinkedIn dominate AI citations. A brand's owned properties on these platforms may be as important as their website for AI search visibility.

**5. Senior SEO skills are premium.** With 59% of jobs senior-level, the market is valuing strategy, AI fluency, and cross-channel integration over tactical execution. Investing in skill development is critical.
