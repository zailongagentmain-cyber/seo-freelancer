# AI Search Reputation Management: How to Control Your Brand Narrative in Perplexity, ChatGPT, and Gemini

**The rules of brand reputation have changed.** For 25 years, managing your online reputation meant optimizing for Google — controlling what appeared on Page 1, pushing down negative results, building review volume on Google and Yelp. That game is not over, but it's no longer the only game in town.

We are now living in the era of AI answer engines. Google's own AI Overviews appear in the majority of informational searches. Perplexity has 10 million-plus active users. ChatGPT with web browsing cites sources directly in its answers. Gemini is pre-installed on Samsung and Google Pixel flagship devices by default. And when someone asks an AI engine "Is [your brand] trustworthy?" or "Should I use [your product]?" — the answer that AI gives can reach millions of users who never click through to your website.

**AI Search Reputation Management (AISRM)** is the discipline of auditing, shaping, and actively building your brand's narrative across AI answer engines. This article is the complete guide: how AI engines select and cite sources, what determines whether your brand is portrayed accurately and favorably, and the exact playbook to audit, repair, and build your AI reputation.

## The New Reputation Battlefield — AI Answer Engines

The shift is already done. It's not coming — it's here. Google AI Overviews now appear in the majority of informational searches across categories from health to finance to product research. Perplexity reported 10 million active users in 2025. ChatGPT reached 180 million weekly active users in early 2026. Gemini comes pre-installed on the two largest Android device ecosystems — Samsung Galaxy S series and Google Pixel.

The reputation game has changed. It's no longer just about what appears on Page 1 of Google. It's about what AI says about your brand when a user asks:

- "Is [brand name] a good company?"
- "Should I use [product] or [competitor]?"
- "What do reviews say about [brand]?"
- "Who founded [brand] and is it legitimate?"

These queries — brand comparison queries, trust assessment queries, purchase consideration queries — are increasingly answered by AI engines before the user ever clicks anything. The AI's answer is the experience. Your website may never be visited.

AISRM is distinct from traditional ORM (Online Reputation Management) in one critical way: **ORM targets human readers who are actively searching for your brand. AISRM targets AI engines that are proactively describing your brand to users who may not have heard of you yet.** You need both.

## How AI Engines Actually Select and Cite Sources

AI engines don't cite randomly, and they don't cite everyone. The citation process has four stages:

**1. Retrieval — Finding Candidate Documents**

AI engines use traditional information retrieval (web crawlers, sitemaps, feed subscriptions) to build a pool of candidate documents for a given query. This is closest to traditional SEO crawling. If your site isn't crawled regularly, it won't appear at all in AI candidate pools.

**2. Ranking — Scoring Source Authority**

Once candidates are identified, the AI model scores them by:

- **Authority**: How recognized is this source in this topic area? Citation patterns from other authoritative sources matter heavily here. If Gartner, Harvard Business Review, or TechCrunch cites you, AI engines notice.
- **Relevance**: How well does this source's content match the specific sub-question within the user's query?
- **Freshness**: AI engines strongly prefer recent content. A 2026 article beats a 2022 article for the same query.
- **Trust signals**: HTTPS, clear authorship, consistent entity information across sources, absence of spam signals.

**3. Synthesis — Selecting Extractable Passages**

This is the step most brands miss. AI engines don't rank pages — they extract **passages** that answer specific sub-questions within a query. If your content doesn't have a clear, self-contained passage that answers a sub-question, it won't be selected regardless of how well your page ranks in Google.

Microsoft described it directly: AI "breaks content down into smaller, structured pieces that can be evaluated for authority and relevance. Those pieces are then assembled into answers."

**4. Attribution — Deciding Which Source Gets Cited**

For each specific claim in an AI answer, the engine selects the highest-authority source that supports that claim. This is why you can see a single AI answer citing five different sources — each for a different claim within the answer.

**The implication**: You don't need to rank #1 on Google to be cited by AI. You need a clear, authoritative passage that answers a specific sub-question better than competing sources. First-cited sources in AI answers receive 3x more referral traffic than second-cited sources (University of Toronto, September 2025).

## The AISRM Audit — What Does AI Actually Say About Your Brand?

Before you can manage your AI reputation, you need to audit it. Here's the comprehensive AISRM audit process:

### Step 1: Search Inventory — Map All AI Engines

Create a spreadsheet and test these AI engines for every major brand query:

| AI Engine | URL | Notes |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| Perplexity | perplexity.ai | Default (free) mode |
| ChatGPT | chatgpt.com | Web browsing enabled |
| Gemini | gemini.google.com | Default |
| Claude | claude.ai | Web search enabled |
| Brave Leo | brave.com/leo | Browser-integrated |
| Microsoft Copilot | copilot.microsoft.com | Bing-powered |
| Google AI Overviews | google.com | Check via VPN for global |

### Step 2: Query Your Brand Across These Categories

For each engine, test queries in these categories:

- Brand awareness: "[Brand name] — what is it?"
- Trust assessment: "Is [brand name] legitimate / trustworthy / a scam?"
- Product comparison: "[Brand] vs [Competitor] — which is better?"
- Review/sentiment: "What do reviews say about [brand name]?"
- Purchase intent: "Best [product category] with [brand name]"
- News/recent: "[Brand name] news 2026"

### Step 3: Citation Tracking

For each query where your brand appears (or should appear), document:

- Is your brand cited? Y/N
- What specific claim is attributed to you?
- Is the cited information accurate and up-to-date?
- Is a competitor cited more prominently?
- What is the sentiment (positive, neutral, negative)?

### Step 4: Gap Analysis

For each category query where your brand should appear but doesn't:

- What competitor is cited instead?
- What sub-question is the competitor's passage answering well?
- Does your site have content that could answer that sub-question?

### Step 5: Claim Accuracy Audit

For every AI citation of your brand:

- Is the quoted information factually correct?
- Are statistics, prices, or specifications accurate?
- Is the context favorable or unfavorable?

## The Zero-Citation Problem — Why Great Brands Get Ignored by AI

Many reputable brands with strong Google rankings are conspicuously absent from AI citations. Here's why:

**Format mismatch**: Your homepage or About page provides overview information. It doesn't answer specific sub-questions. AI engines decompose queries into sub-questions — "Who founded X?" "Does X have good customer service?" "Is X worth the price?" — and your overview content doesn't speak directly to these.

**Missing FAQ content**: Brands without Q&A-formatted content are harder for AI to cite with precision. If you don't explicitly ask and answer the questions users have, AI engines can't extract your answer.

**Low entity clarity**: If AI engines can't clearly determine what your brand is, who runs it, and how it differs from competitors, they default to citing clearer sources.

**No third-party reinforcement**: AI engines heavily weight earned media. If no credible publication has ever mentioned your brand, AI can't cite you with confidence. It needs cross-source verification.

**Thin product pages**: Product pages that list features without comprehensive expert commentary don't give AI enough extractable content. You need substantive, authoritative content — not just specifications.

**Wikipedia absence**: Wikipedia is the single most-cited source by AI engines. If your brand has no Wikipedia presence (and isn't mentioned on major Wikipedia-adjacent reference sites), AI engines have limited trust signals to draw from.

## E-E-A-T as AI Trust Infrastructure

E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) was designed for Google's quality raters. AI engines have adopted similar signals because they're the best available proxies for content quality:

**Experience**: "Has this person actually used the product or experienced the topic?" AI engines strongly favor first-hand accounts — reviews, case studies, personal narratives. "I tested Product X for six months and here is what happened" beats "Product X is a good product." For brand AISRM, having your customers' genuine experiences cited is among the highest-value signals you can build.

**Expertise**: Formal credentials, domain expertise, professional background. AI can verify expertise from author bios, publication history, and institutional affiliations. Make your team's expertise visible and verifiable.

**Authoritativeness**: How recognized is this source in this specific topic area? Citation patterns from other authoritative sources are the key signal here. If industry publications, analysts, or trusted media cite your brand, AI engines treat those as endorsements.

**Trustworthiness**: Accurate, verifiable facts. Consistent information across all sources. No spam signals. Secure site (HTTPS). Clear privacy policy, contact information, and about page.

For AISRM specifically: your brand's E-E-A-T signals must be **visible, verifiable, and consistent** across your website, Wikipedia, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, Wikidata, and major industry directories. AI engines cross-reference these sources constantly. Inconsistency — different founding dates, different headquarters, different product claims — is an immediate trust penalty.

## Proactive AISRM — Building Your AI Citation Profile

AISRM isn't just damage control. The most effective strategy is actively building your AI citation profile:

**Create claim-specific content**: Write content that directly answers the specific sub-questions AI engines extract. Don't just write "What is Brand X?" Write "Who founded Brand X and what are their credentials?" Write "What do real users say about Brand X after one year of use?" Each question needs its own answer-optimized passage.

**Earn third-party citations**: Guest posts, industry coverage, podcast appearances, award mentions. AI engines heavily weight earned media. A PR strategy is an AISRM strategy. Target publications that AI engines particularly trust: Harvard Business Review, Gartner, Forrester, TechCrunch, Wired, The Verge, and industry-specific trade publications.

**Optimize for passage retrieval**: Write short, self-contained paragraphs that answer one specific question each. Front-load the answer. No preamble. Structure: "Yes, [Brand] does [X]. Here's why it matters: [direct answer]." AI engines extract this format with near-perfect accuracy.

**Build entity clarity**: Ensure your brand's Name, Address, Phone, Service/Offerings, and founding information are consistent and complete across your website, Google Business Profile, Wikidata, Wikipedia, Crunchbase, LinkedIn Company Page, and major industry directories. AI engines use cross-reference consistency as a primary trust signal.

**Get cited in AI-preferred sources**: AI engines have their own ranking of which sources they prefer to cite. Getting mentioned in Gartner, Forrester, HBR, or major industry analyst reports is among the highest-impact AISRM actions because those sources are in AI engines' trusted citation pools.

**Wikipedia**: Having a Wikipedia article (or being prominently mentioned in one) is one of the highest-value AISRM investments. Wikipedia's editorial standards and third-party citations make it one of the most trusted sources in every AI engine's citation system.

## Reactive AISRM — Repairing Negative or Inaccurate AI Citations

When AI engines cite incorrect, outdated, or negative information about your brand, here's how to respond:

**Inaccurate fact citations**: If AI says "Brand X has a 2.3-star rating" and that rating is wrong, the fastest fix is to publish accurate information on your own site in the exact format AI can extract. Publish an accurate, current rating roundup with clear numbers and sourcing. AI will re-crawl and update its citation within weeks to months.

**Negative framing**: If AI consistently cites negative reviews or articles, the counter is quantity and quality of positive third-party coverage. You cannot directly edit what AI engines say, but you can give them more and better positive material to cite. A proactive PR and content strategy over 3-6 months will shift AI citation patterns.

**Outdated information**: Publish clearly dated, labeled "current state" content that directly supersedes old information. If AI cites a 2022 article about your company's old product, create a 2026 article that opens with "As of 2026, [Company] has [updated facts]" and link to it prominently.

**Wikipedia corrections**: Wikipedia reflects AI training data. Corrections to Wikipedia articles take time — Wikipedia's processes are slow by design — but are one of the highest-impact AISRM actions for fixing AI inaccuracies because Wikipedia is cited by all major AI engines.

**Google Business Profile**: Keep your GBP complete, accurate, and updated. For local brand queries ("Is [restaurant chain] near me open?"), your GBP is the primary AI citation source.

**The relying party problem**: If AI engines are incorrectly citing a third-party article, you can reach out to that publisher to request corrections. You cannot directly request AI engine corrections — there is no AI engine correction request portal. The solution is always fixing the source.

## Monitoring Your AI Brand Presence

AISRM is not a one-time project — it's ongoing. Set up systematic monitoring:

**Weekly AI brand queries**: Manually run your top 10 brand queries across Perplexity, ChatGPT, and Gemini. Track what changes from week to week. This takes 20 minutes and gives you an early warning system.

**Google Search Console AI Overview report**: New in 2026, GSC shows which queries trigger AI Overviews and your site's presence in those. Check this weekly — it's free data you already have access to.

**Alert systems**: Set up Google Alerts for "[Brand name] + AI" and "[Brand name] + chatbot" and "[Brand name] + Perplexity." When your brand appears in AI-related news or analysis, investigate immediately.

**Share of Voice in AI**: Track how often your brand vs. competitors appears in AI answers for category queries. This is the new "SOV" (Share of Voice) metric. Create a simple tracking spreadsheet and update monthly.

**Third-party AI monitoring tools**: Tools like Canary, Brandwatch, and Mention are adding AI engine monitoring features as of 2026. These are worth the investment as they mature. Canary in particular focuses specifically on AI search visibility tracking.

**Quarterly full AISRM audit**: Run the complete Chapter 3 audit every quarter. Track your progress over time: are you appearing in more AI answers? Are competitor citations decreasing for your priority queries?

## AISRM by Business Type

**E-commerce brands**: AI frequently answers "Is [product] worth it?" with citations from product reviews and comparison articles. Optimize your product pages for passage retrieval (detailed, first-hand experience descriptions). Create comparison content vs. competitors. Get cited in buying guide roundups on major publication sites.

**SaaS / B2B**: AI engines answer "Best [category] software for [use case]" by citing review platforms and comparison content. Create "Best [category] software" content on your own site. Ensure your G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, and product review profiles are complete — AI engines cite these platforms heavily for SaaS comparisons.

**Local businesses**: For "Is [business] good?" queries, AI engines cite Google Business Profile, Yelp, and local review aggregators. Maintain consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone) everywhere. Respond to reviews promptly. Keep photos and hours current.

**Media brands and publishers**: AI cites news articles directly. Ensure articles are dated, bylined, and have clear expert sources attributed. Structured NewsArticle schema is essential. AI engines will not cite anonymous or undated news content.

**Personal brands and creators**: LinkedIn, personal website, podcast appearances, and earned media are your AISRM trifecta. Entity consistency matters more here than for any other type — Wikipedia, LinkedIn, personal website, and podcast platform profiles must all tell a consistent story with consistent facts.

## The 30-Day AISRM Action Plan

**Days 1-5 — Audit**: Run your complete AISRM audit (Chapter 3). Document every AI engine's portrayal of your brand for your top 10 queries. Identify the top 3 problems: what queries should you appear in but don't? What incorrect information is being cited?

**Days 6-10 — Fix your own site**: Create FAQ content answering the specific questions AI engines are failing to answer about your brand. Optimize existing content for passage retrieval (Chapter 4). Ensure all content has clear author attribution with credentials visible.

**Days 11-15 — E-E-A-T signals**: Update author bios across your site. Verify entity consistency across your website, Google Business Profile, Wikidata, Wikipedia, Crunchbase, and LinkedIn. Every piece of information should cross-reference consistently.

**Days 16-20 — Earned media**: Launch outreach targeting 3-5 industry publications or podcasts where credible citations of your brand would appear. If you don't have a Wikipedia presence, start that process — it's the single highest-value AISRM investment for most brands.

**Days 21-25 — Wikipedia and directories**: Submit Wikipedia corrections if needed. Update your Google Business Profile and all critical NAP directories. Ensure every piece of third-party information about your brand tells a consistent, accurate story.

**Days 26-30 — Set up monitoring**: Create your weekly AI brand query tracker. Set up Google Alerts. Document your findings in a shareable AISRM report for your team or stakeholders. Schedule your next quarterly audit.
