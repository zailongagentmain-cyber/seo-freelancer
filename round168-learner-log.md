## What Was Learned (Key Insights)

1. **Fragment > Page**: AI selects content fragments, not pages. Every section of a page is independently evaluated for citation fitness.

2. **Earned media dominates in AI**: University of Toronto study — 92.1% third-party citations in AI vs 54.1% in Google for consumer electronics. Digital PR is now an SEO tactic.

3. **Training cutoffs ≠ uniform across platforms**: GPT-4o (Oct 2023) vs GPT-5 (Aug 2025) vs Gemini (Jan 2025). A large share of AI responses draw from parametric memory with zero attribution. This makes "brand mention" optimization as important as "own-domain content" optimization.

4. **Q&A format is the native AI citation unit**: Microsoft said AI can "lift these word for word." Content structured as explicit Q&A pairs is maximally extractable.

5. **Tabs/collapsibles = citation death**: Microsoft explicitly warns against hiding answers in expandable menus for AI. This is a specific technical actionability finding.

6. **Web Guide's query fan-out creates multiple ranking surfaces per query**: In a single Web Guide SERP, 6-8 different domains can win for the same query, each serving a different sub-intent. Optimizing for head terms is no longer enough.

7. **AI visibility dashboards are unreliable**: "Bullshit with a confidence interval." The article (Define Media Group, Roger Montti) is a must-read for any SEO still buying GEO dashboard subscriptions.

8. **"Florida-style" reset risk is real but not certain**: Conditions mirror pre-Panda (scaled low-quality content at AI velocity), but Google's rolling systems have been catching it. The spam update completing in 20 hours (fastest ever) without community impact signals Google may be using something bigger next time.

9. **GBP = live engagement surface, not directory**: Hours are now the #5 local ranking factor. Post weekly. Fresh photos monthly. Review velocity matters more than review volume.

10. **Wikipedia's AI ban = originality premium validation**: If the world's largest open encyclopedia rejects AI content, commercial sites should expect similar quality pressures from Google's Helpful Content System.
