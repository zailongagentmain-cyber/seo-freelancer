### Key Findings Synthesized

#### 1. The Agentic Web Has Arrived
Google announced a new "Google-Agent" user agent (March 2026). WebMCP lets AI agents interact with website backends natively — filling forms, executing transactions, negotiating prices — without simulating human browsing. Liz Reid (Head of Search) confirmed agents will "talk to each other." The human-first web era is ending.

#### 2. Five AI Agent Protocols Every SEO Must Know
- **MCP (Model Context Protocol)**: Agents access backend data securely (CRM, inventory, pricing)
- **A2A (Agent2Agent)**: Bot-to-bot communication and transactions
- **UCP (Universal Commerce Protocol)**: Machines buy products directly from SERPs — no site visit required
- **A2UI (Agent to User Interface)**: Agents compose new visual layouts for users in real time
- **AG-UI (Agent User Interaction)**: Middleware for streaming real-time AI data

#### 3. AI Selects Fragments, Not Pages
Microsoft's description: AI "breaks content down into smaller, structured pieces that can be evaluated for authority and relevance. Those pieces are then assembled into answers." Your page can rank #1 on Google and never appear in an AI response if content isn't structured in extractable fragments.

#### 4. AI Citation Statistics
- AI traffic: 1.08% of all website sessions, growing ~1%/month
- AI referrals spiked 357% YoY (Microsoft, June 2025) = 1.13 billion visits
- 1 in 4 Google searches triggers AI Overview; in healthcare, ~1 in 2

#### 5. What Actually Gets Cited (Research-Backed)
- University of Toronto: AI cites 3rd-party earned media 92.1% (consumer electronics) vs Google's 54.1%
- GEO Study: citing credible sources → 115.1% AI visibility increase for non-top-position pages
- Carnegie Mellon AutoGEO: up to 50.99% improvement with optimal structure
- GEO-16: metadata/freshness, semantic HTML, structured data = top 3 on-page factors
- Columbia/MIT: 10 of 15 common rewriting heuristics produced zero or negative results

#### 6. Content Structure That Earns Citations
- Descriptive heading hierarchy (one idea per H2/H3)
- Q&A format (questions as headings with direct answers)
- Front-load answers (key info first, then context)
- Self-contained sections (fragment must make sense alone)
- No hidden content in tabs/accordions
- Bulleted/numbered lists for clean extraction
- Comparison tables for structured data
